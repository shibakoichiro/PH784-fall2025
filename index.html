About the Course

Methodological rigor is essential in social epidemiology to improve our understanding of the social determinants of health and to inform interventions and policies that promote health equity. Advancing methodological rigor in social epidemiologic research requires an understanding of the unique challenges and key research questions in this field.

This course covers the primary considerations needed to critically evaluate evidence from the social epidemiologic literature and to identify gaps for future research. We will focus on causal estimands, interpretation, and underlying assumptions rather than the statistical mechanics of analytic methods. This is not intended as a general epidemiologic methods course; rather, it provides an overview of recent discussions on methodological issues specific to social epidemiology.

Logistics

  • Location: Boston University School of Public Health (CTC 460)
  • Format: In-person lectures and weekly problem sets
    • Throughout the course, students will use Quarto to complete assignments. Although data analysis tasks using R are minimal, students are encouraged to become familiar with both R and Quarto for reproducible data analysis and reporting.
