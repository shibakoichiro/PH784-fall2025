Read the following article and answer the questions.

  • Li S, Stampfer MJ, Williams DR, VanderWeele TJ. Association of religious service attendance with mortality among women. JAMA internal medicine. 2016 Jun 1;176(6):777-85.
    • https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamainternalmedicine/fullarticle/2521827

Briefly, this study estimated various “effects” of religious service attendance on mortality. They used data from the Nurses’ Health Study, a large prospective cohort study among US women. They examined both all-cause mortality and cause-specific mortality as outcomes, but we will focus on their findings for all-cause mortality.

They estimated the effects of religious service attendance in 1996 (\(A_{1996}\)) and 2000 (\(A_{2000}\)) on all-cause mortality between 2000-2012 (\(Y_{2000-2012}\)). They controlled for various covariates, including sociodemographic characteristics and health conditions in 1992 (\(L_{1992}\)) and 1996 (\(L_{1996}\)), as well as pre-baseline religious service attendance in 1992 (\(A_{1992}\)), using a method called a “marginal structural model.” Suppose their analysis is based on the DAG below.

Table 2 of the paper shows results of this analysis. For example, the estimate for “At least once per week” in 1996 and “More than once a week” in 2000 was HR = 0.50 (95% CI: 0.46-0.54). This corresponds to the following causal estimand.

\(HR=h(t)^{a_{1996}=1,a_{2000}=1} / h(t)^{a_{1996}=0,a_{2000}=0}\)

where \(t\) is time to the onset of \(Y_{2000-2012}\).
